# Honours Thesis

**Title:** Muscle Coactivation Levels Reflect a Trade-off Between Metabolic Cost and Performance When
Responding to Physical Disturbances During Upper Limb Posture Control
**Author:** Bhuvan Dubey
**Supervisor:** Dr. Tyler Cluff

[Read the thesis (PDF)](./Honours-Thesis-FINAL.pdf) · [Word source](./Honours-Thesis-FINAL.docx)

## Summary

The full honours thesis writeup, extending the [proposal](../Honours-Proposal) and
[poster](../Poster) into a complete report. Twelve participants performed an upper-limb posture-control
task in a Kinarm exoskeleton robot while muscle coactivation was manipulated (0.5x–16x their
self-selected level) using EMG biofeedback, with metabolic cost measured via gas respirometry and
behavioural performance via error rate. Results show that participants prioritize metabolic
efficiency over performance at self-selected coactivation levels, but biofeedback-driven increases in
coactivation improve performance (lower error rates, smaller elbow displacement, faster movement
time) at the cost of higher metabolic expenditure — consistent with coactivation playing a role in
priming the nervous system for sensory feedback processing, not just mechanical stiffening.
