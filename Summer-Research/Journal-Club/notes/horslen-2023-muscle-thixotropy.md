# Horslen et al. (2023) — History-dependent muscle resistance to stretch remains high after small, posturally relevant pre-movements

**Journal:** Journal of Experimental Biology, 226(18), jeb245456
**DOI:** [10.1242/jeb.245456](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.245456)

## Introduction
- **Research question/hypothesis:** How do prior movements affect a muscle's resistance to subsequent
  stretch? Hypothesis: larger and faster "conditioning" stretches reduce muscle resistance to a following
  test stretch (history dependence / muscle thixotropy).
- **Motivation:** Balance impairments are associated with increased postural sway. The authors wanted to
  test whether muscle mechanical properties (not just neural control) contribute to that relationship.

## Methods
- Isolated, permeabilized single muscle fibers (rat soleus), with a paired conditioning–test stretch
  paradigm: a triangular conditioning stretch–shorten cycle followed by a ramp-and-hold test stretch.
- Conditioning stretch amplitude and inter-stretch interval (ISI) were systematically varied across low,
  intermediate, and high activation levels.
- Outcome measures: short-range stiffness (SRS, first 0.02 s of the test stretch) and impulse (area under
  the stress-time curve over the 0.084 s ramp).

## Results
- History-dependent reductions in muscle resistance to stretch occurred with larger/faster conditioning
  stretches and shorter ISIs — but resistance stayed near 100% (i.e., no reduction) for small,
  posturally-relevant stretches (<1% fiber length) at ISIs >0.1–1 s, consistent with healthy quiet standing.
- The reduction was largest at intermediate activation levels (a U-shaped relationship with activation).

## Discussion
- Increased/faster postural sway (as seen in balance-impaired individuals) would push the muscle into the
  amplitude/ISI regime where resistance to stretch drops — reducing the mechanical (non-neural) contribution
  to balance and shifting more of the burden onto neural feedback.
- Muscle movement history and its interaction with the CNS jointly shape balance control, with implications
  for rehabilitation in individuals with balance impairments.

## Notes / things to remember
- Time window for SRS: first 0.02 s of the test stretch (instantaneous — muscle activation right at
  stretch onset).
- Impulse window: 0–0.084 s (duration of the ramp/lengthening phase).
- This is an *in vitro*, isolated single-muscle-fiber preparation — not *in vivo*.
